Trauma is the kind of concept and word that often gets just kind of thrown around in conversations. like, i'd personally hear pretty frequently growing up, "that situation was so traumatic" or "that dude gave me trauma". It's often enough to the point that i'd argue in today's culture, its a concept that has gone kind of misvalued and misunderstood. 
It's such an *abstract*, yet, **heavy** concept, and growing up and hearing about this word was kind of confusing, because I'd hear it in so many different contexts. 
But of course, I grew up, and throughout my life I've experienced a lot of different kinds of pain. With feeling pain, I naturally needed some way to identify it and work through it. So, I looked into what it all meant, and what trauma actually is.
Trauma is basically unresolved pain.
One day I was riding my skateboard, and I decided to send myself down this pretty huge ramp. It wasn't until a quarter of the way down the ramp, that I realized there was some mild construction at the bottom, and the floor was littered with a bunch of rocks. I couldn't really stop myself from going down the ramp, I was moving too fast at this point, and so I just had to eat whatever was coming for me. My skateboard hit the rocks, and flew out from under me. My whole body lurched forward, and my knees and arms slammed into the ground and slid across the rocks and concrete. 
I didn't feel anything for a good, about, 5 seconds, as I sat upright and tried to get my bearings. And then the pain settled in, and I looked down at my palms and kneecaps and saw some big tears and bruises. I sat there for like a good ten minutes, just kind of in half disbelief of the pain I was feeling, letting my body heal a bit so my legs would hurt less and allow me to get up again, and thinking about how its gonna suck having these scabs for the next little while. Of course, I then got up and continued skating. 
This is an example of physical trauma. I didn't experience much mental pain, but notice how although the event of me eating shit on the concrete only occurred during like a ten second window, I had to pause for a good little while afterward to let my body heal. My body experienced pain, but that pain didn't just immediately go away, I had to take the time sit there and let the wounds heal. 
*Just* experiencing and getting through a painful event doesn't necessarily mean you're done. If you pick up some scars, you still have a lot of responsibility to make sure the scars heal properly. If you get cut up, you can put on some bandaids. If you get a bruise, you need to make sure it stays clean. But keep in mind that *postponing* a scar from healing is just as easy. Obviously, not treating a wound regularly or not keeping it clean increases the chance that it'll get worse. And for some scars, if they aren't treated properly, it can lead to permanent effects.
By now, you can probably guess that im trying to draw a parallel here. Physical trauma has a lot of similarities with mental trauma. 
Just because you already went through the experience of someone emotionally hurting you, that doesn't mean that it's over. There's still some work to be done. Much like how I took 10 minutes to sit down and assess my wounds, and how I made sure over the next week or so to make sure the wounds stayed clean, you need to conciously make sure your mental wounds are treated and healing properly. 
Physical wounds that you had years ago are probably healed by now, right? Your body doesn't worry about those old wounds because they are already dealt with. 
The same goes for mental wounds. When you have an open mental wound, your brain struggles to try and repair it.
Mental wounds that are still open will affect your beliefs, will slowly morph your subconcious, and will influence the way you feel and act without you noticing.
Mental wounds are a little different in the sense that you need to *conciously heal them*. For more info on what happens if you don't conciously heal a mental wound, read my other journal "*if you don't heal from victimhood you become the abuser*". 
Good news is, is that once mental wounds are healed, you don't really have to worry about it anymore. In fact, that's one of the main goals when trying to heal from emotional trauma.
If you think of a traumatic memory, and you still feel a pit in your stomach or your brain go a little bit panicked, then chances are that that mental wound is still not fully healed. Your brain is focusing on the open wound again and feeling the pain that comes with it.
The result you want to get to, is to be able to think of a traumatic memory, and not get those feelings of stress.
I have had many memories, that at one point, felt so hard to think about because of how much of an affect those wounds had on my mind. Most of those memories I've fully worked through, and now I'm at the point where I can think of an event that hurt me a lot, and it just kind of feels like another memory to me. Like, yeah, that happened to me. It sucked, but I don't really feel much about it because it doesn't really affect me anymore. 
You wanna get to the point where thinking of the traumatic memory makes you feel like you're watching someone else go through it, like in a movie. It's like, "yeah, that event was objectively sad." But you don't really feel *too* crazy about it, cause it's like you're just watching it, you're not *in* it anymore. 
When you get to that point, that's how you know you've worked through the trauma. 
It's also good to leverage these wounds in a way to help you grow. Mental pain can aid you and give you a lot of good beliefs, that if handled in the right way, can help you grow so much in life. Just like how the controlled, physical tears in your muscles hurt you when you lift weights, but come back to serve you in the future as you heal those wounds with stronger muscles.
Trauma is unresolved pain. In a later journal, I'll talk more specifically about *how* to heal trauma, and what it can do for ya.
