Hello, World! <br>
As you can see, i made this pretty neat website. <br>
Everything you see here is made up of the three programming languages websites are built on, HTML, CSS, and Javascript, all with code that I personally hand typed every single letter for.
I also thought up and coded every simulation that you see running in the background of several of the pages, including the boids simulation you see behind this journal right now!<br>
This website was a pretty big challenge for me because I knew practically ZERO html, css, or javascript going into it... I've learned like everything along the way by looking at maybe 2 youtube tutorials and TONS of documentation.
I'd say making each of the simulations was probably the most fun part. I'm a programmer by nature, and nothing else puts me in a flow state quite like sitting in front of my computer and typing out silly lines so my computer can do silly calculations. Javascript, although notoriously being a language that makes your brain hurt a bit, came kind of naturally to me as someone who has already made projects with python, lua, gdscript, and c#.<br>
However, all the time in my life taken from being in front of a computer screen has come with the downside of my artistic skills not being as up to par as my programming skills. I guess that's part of why I wanted to work on this website. <br>
I like to think of html and css as being one of the most straightforward mediums of programmatic aesthetic art. And by that I mean... Hmm, let me put it this way. Usually when I program something cool, and I show somebody, I show them what the code does, and then I show them the big wall of text that I wrote to make it. And from a programmer's perspective (my perspective), that big wall of text is really cool! And just the code by itself can be really beautiful to me. <br> 
However, to the sorry victim that I've chosen to make sit through my nerdy interests, they see just that: a big wall of text. My point being, with html and css, everything you see on the website IS the huge block of text that I've written. Instead it's just, moved around and stuff. and colored and stuff. And that, that can be much more easily appreciated by a non-programmer.<br>
Still, things like the javascript that does all of the boids in the background and all the stars on the main page go unnappreciated in the sense that its hard for a non-programmer to understand the beauty of it..<br>
However! Maybe thats how things ought to go sometimes. For some things, in order to truly understand the beauty of it, you have to dive into it. Explore it. See what it's all about. Experience for yourself how in depth some of these arbitrary systems are that some fellow humans made, just for you to come along and mess it all up! But just maybe you'll mess it up in a cool way, and all the effort will be worth it.
