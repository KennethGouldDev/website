Hello, World! <br>
As you can see, i made this pretty neat website. <br>
Everything you see here is made up of the basic three programming languages websites are built on: HTML, CSS, and Javascript. All with code that *I personally* hand typed every single letter for.
I also thought up and coded every simulation that you see running in the background of several of the pages, including the boids simulation you see behind this journal right now!<br>
This website was a pretty big challenge for me, because I knew practically ZERO html, css, or javascript going into it... I've learned like everything along the way by looking at maybe 2 youtube tutorials and TONS of documentation.<br>
I'd say making each of the simulations was probably the most fun part. I'm a programmer by nature, and nothing else puts me in a flow state quite like sitting in front of my computer and typing out silly lines so my computer can do silly calculations. <br>
Javascript, although notoriously being a language that makes your brain hurt a bit, came kind of naturally to me as someone who has already made projects with python, lua, gdscript, and c#.<br>
All the time i've spent being in front of a computer screen has come with the upside of my programming being great, but with the downside of my artistic skills not being *as* up to par. I guess that's a big reason why I wanted to work on this website! <br>
I personally think of html and css as being one of the most straightforward mediums of programmatic aesthetic art. And by that I mean... Hmm, let me put it this way. Usually when I program something cool and want to show someone, I show them what the code does, and then I show them the big wall of text that I wrote to make it. And from a programmer's perspective (my perspective), that big wall of text is really cool! Sometime's i don't even need to see what it ends up doing. Just the code by itself can be really beautiful to me. <br> 
However, to the sorry victim that I've chosen to make sit through my nerdy interests, they see just that: a big wall of text. And that brings me to my point: with html and css, its a bit different. *Everything* you see on the website **IS** the huge block of text that I've written. It's just, moved around and stuff. and colored and stuff. <br> Being able to see much more directly what a file of code does, can be much more easily appreciated by a non-programmer.<br>
Still, things like the javascript that does all of the boids in the background and all the stars on the main page go unnappreciated in the sense that its hard for a non-programmer to understand the beauty of it..<br>
However! I argue that at the same time, that's how things ought to go. For some things, in order to truly understand the beauty of it, you *have* to dive into it. **Explore** it. See what it's all about. <br>Take a look at these arbitrary systems some fellow humans made, and experience it for yourself. Chances are, you'll mess everything up! But *just maybe* you'll mess it up in a cool way. Those cool mess-ups are what makes it all beautiful, and all worth it.
