Gratitude journaling is a great practice! I've been doing it for many years. Journaling in general is *really* good. Well hey, haha, look at that, im journaling right now, on my journals page. Wow.<br>
But more specifically, I want to talk about gratitude journaling. I started doing it around, what, early junior year of highschool? so around 3 or 4 years ago as of writing this. And yes, over the past few years, it has most definitely improved my life. <br>
The practice is really simple. There are so many ways to do it, but this specific method has stuck with me personally the most: <br>
When I wake up, I (ideally) first thing in the morning, I grab the journal next to my bed, write the day's date, and then two sentences about something that I am grateful for.<br>
What I write can be incredibly simple. I like to use the format "I am grateful for (blank) because (blank)".<br>
That's literally it. Some days I'll write more that just two sentences, some day's I'll just forget, but I try to stick to that baseline as much as possible. Like, I think I've wrote about 10 times in my life "I am grateful for my bed because its so comfortable and sleep is so good i could fall asleep any mo"<br>
Its very incredibly simple and takes up a minute of my time, but the positive effects it can have on your life are outstanding!!<br>
First of all, doing it first thing in the morning sets you up pretty nicely, because it trains your brain to think of something positive immediately after you wake up. You can imagine how good of a habit that is to get into, right? Imagine you wake up, doesn't matter what day it is or wherever you are, the first thing you think of is what you're happy about, and you're already starting the day with a smile on your face. <br>
I think thats pretty cool. And besides just doing it in the morning, gratitude in general is just a great practice.<br>
I've talked to a lot of people and helped a lot of people who were going through sad times. I've especially pulled *myself* out of those dips. Whenever I talk to people who you can just kind of sense don't have a lot of gratitude in their lives, or even myself when I'm going through a mental low, it's kind of amazing how it's actually pretty difficult for them or myself to think of something positive. <br>
That's the real thing you're training for here. Thinking of something good is actually a *skill*. If you've gone your whole life *rarely* ever thinking of what you have that you're happy about, it's going to be quite difficult to be happy about things. I've mentioned gratitude journaling to people before, and I've genuinely heard the response "I don't know, I don't really have anything to be grateful for." <br>
Bro are you serious!! *Nothing to be grateful for!!??*<br>
**Every morning that you wake up, you have air in your lungs.**<br>
That alone is a gift we are all given that we seldom appreciate. <br>
What about that bus driver that shows up to drive you every morning? What about that waterbottle that has been with you through every workout? What about the friends you get the opportunity to talk to and experience life with? What about the computer desk holding up your monitor? What about that specific tree that you see every morning when you look out the window?<br>
I've been grateful for a lot of trees. No joke, like, there are multiple trees that I would see every day, and would write about pretty often. <br>
I'd write about how appreciative I was that every time I got up, no matter how I felt, I could see that one tree in that same spot waiting to greet me. When it was fall and the leaves would turn yellow, I'd write about how I'm grateful for the tree's resilience, and how beautiful it is being able to witness its various stages as it grew, and how I was glad it could watch me grow with each day as well. <br>
Anyway, back to what I was talking about, gratitude is a skill, and all skills can be trained. <br>
One day in the future, you'll probably catch yourself in a sad time. When your brain is working against you, it might feel like darkness is encompassing you, and it might feel like nothing in the world is right. In most of those situations, it's gonna be up to *you* to take you out of there. One of the biggest stepping stones to get out of there is working *really hard* to think of something good. And trust me, in those times, it really does feel impossible to think of somnething positive. <br>
However, if your past self put in the reps into practicing thinking of what you're grateful for, it's gonna be a lot easier to pull yourself out of a depression. That's the opportunity that you have here by gratitude journaling, you can give your future self the gift of a healthy mind, a strong mind that can stay optimistic and resilient no matter what. <br>
So yeah, this is where I do a call to action or whatever, gratitude journaling is really great. Try it out!!
