what is upp yes the title pretty much explains itself.<br>
There was a point in my life in which I basically had no friends. It was around the junior year of highschool, and I really felt like I had nobody. This time was a really low point in my life, and much like every low point in my life, I felt a big desire to change that.<br> 
But like, what do you do when you have no friends? I mean, actually the answer is pretty simple, you just go out and make friends, But to my brain, it was like, I can't just go into a problem without knowing anything else about it. I need some sort of strategy, some kind of plan of attack so that I can minimize the possibility of stumbling around looking stupid doing something I have no clue about. <br> 
So, I decided to read up. What makes this a funny fact is the fact that reading, itself, is more of an introverted, more secluded activity. So whenever I tell people about this, I usually get a chuckle. <br> 
The first major book on socializing I read (and actually the first self development book I fully finished!) was *How to Win Friends and Influence People by Dale Carnegie.* <br> 
It's a book that's pretty old, as you can probably tell by the slightly jarring title, <span class="quiet">(written 1936!!!)</span> and when you read it there's a lot of big words in there and even sayings that were only used by old people. Despite that, I got through it, and you know what, it actually helped me a ton. How the book is structured, is that most chapters would go over a specific social habit that you could do that will make people like you more. <br> 
One of the ones that still stick with me to this day is to smile. Genuinely tho, smiling is really great and powerful. I've gotten lots of comments before about how I always look happy to meet people and that my smile is cool. heh I love compliments.<br>
Things didn't start out that way, however. Like any other teenage boy I was pretty snarky about the world. If someone came up to me, I'd see it more of an intrusion, or like a "what does this person want from me".<br>
But that's a pretty negative way to look at things. As I started smiling, (and around this time I started gratitude journaling frequently aswell, that probably helped) it's funny, it's like the smile, even if I put it on manually and I wasn't actually too happy at that time, it would inevitably make me *actually* happy.<br>
It's still a habit of mine to this day, whenever I see someone, especially a friend, I kind of instinctively and unconciously smile. And whenever I smile, it also triggers me to think happy thoughts. No matter who it is, whenever I see them or hear from them, (even you! if you're my friend reading this) I always end up thinking a positive thought along the lines of "im really happy that I'm in the presence of this person right now!" or "I'm glad I get to see them and talk to them!"<br>
Anyway, I didn't think I'd talk about smiling that much, but that's *just one* of the many principles that I learned from this book. Isn't that kind of amazing? like, I literally just stared at some text and put it into my mind, and that made me have so many positive experiences and upgraded my life even several years down the line.
Besides just that book though, I also read a ton more books, mostly self help and psychology, and that really helped me understand not just myself, but other people a lot more too. If you're interested, some of my favorites back in the day and even now were:<br>
*The Subtle Art of Not Giving a F###*, *Atomic Habits*, *the Let Them Theory*, *The Way of the Superior Man*<br>
you see, *half* of what I mentioned aren't even social books, they are just about general psychology or self help stuff. But i've really found that figuring out how you yourself work, and how your brain works, will tell you *so so* much about the brains of other people too. Like, we are all different, but we are also all the same.<br>
Wanting to get better at socializing got me into reading psychology, and reading psychology got me better and more interested in socializing, and that kind of became a feedback loop where I just kept learning more and more about myself and other people. <br>
Of course, it's not like *everything* I know is out of a book. What's that saying, "faith without works is dead"? You can think and plan all about how to do something, but you also *actually* have to do it. <span class="quiet">i might write a journal about that later</span><br>
Yeah it's great *knowing* what to do and how to respond in specific situations, but what are ya gonna do about your social anxiety?<br>
In order to break out of my shell, one of the major goals I made for myself was to **get one name a day**. I'd be walking throughout school, and I made it a priority in my mind that I *had* to get a name. Any name. It could be so simple, like "Hey I like your shirt, what's your name? Your name is ####? Alright awesome to meet you ####. Hope to see you later." And I would FIEND over just trying to get that one conversation out every day.<br>
Depending on who you are, that either sounds incredibly simple, or a nightmare. Some days were way easier than others, and some days were definitely harder than others. On days where I wasn't at school, I'd be at the gym. And while I'd do my workout, I'd try to figure out who I could approach and talk to. Again, some of those days were easy, but some of those days I'd be done with my whole workout, and I'd literally be walking around the gym for like several minutes working up the courage to go up to someone and just ask talk to them. It was hard! and kinda weird lol imagining that.<br>
But did it work? It definitely did. Going through that made me a lot more confident, and it demistified the idea of talking to people a lot. After being hurt from people you thought were closest to you, it really isn't easy to get back on your feet socially. However, sitting with the discomfort, working through it and standing up to it, it gives you a reward and an experience that you don't get from much else. Totally worth it, 10/10.<br>
So yeah, if you find yourself in a situation where you need to get back on your feet, figure it out, cause it's awesome! Even if you have to do it in ways that are slightly unorthodox. If it works, it works for you.
